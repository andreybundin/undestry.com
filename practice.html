PRACTICE

Undestry

Independent research and advisory practice.

Undestry is an independent research and advisory practice focused on complex industrial and technology-driven environments.

The work sits between engineering, technology, operations and the decisions made around them. This is where a technical change can affect an operating model, where an information problem can become a management problem, and where a promising technology can look very different once it meets an existing organisation.

Undestry looks at these situations closely and writes about what it finds. The work starts with the subject itself rather than with a predefined solution. Technology matters, but so do the physical systems it serves, the information behind them, the people who operate them and the decisions that depend on them.

Most of the work takes the form of written artefacts. They are meant to be useful on their own: something a reader can pick up between meetings, read on a flight, return to later, or pass to someone else who needs to understand the same subject.

Notes

Notes are short pieces built around an observation that seems worth keeping. An industry can change gradually for a long time before the change becomes obvious. A technology can become important before there is a good name for what is happening. A familiar approach can start producing different results as the environment around it changes. A Note captures such moments. It is not a news item and does not try to cover a subject completely. It records an idea clearly enough to make it useful.

Analytical Briefs

An Analytical Brief takes a defined subject further. Some questions need more than an observation but less than a full research paper. A Brief brings together the relevant context and evidence, follows the subject far enough to establish what matters, and leaves the reader with a clear picture of the situation. The format is deliberately compact. The work behind it may be substantial; the document does not need to show all of it.

Research

Research is for subjects that deserve a longer treatment. These papers examine a subject in depth and develop an argument from evidence rather than from a preferred conclusion. They may cover emerging technologies, changes in industrial systems, organisational patterns or other questions where the consequences are not yet fully understood. The format may resemble a white paper in length and seriousness. The purpose is different. Research is published to make a subject clearer, not to lead the reader towards a product, a platform or a particular commercial decision.

Advisory

Some questions are specific to an organisation and cannot be properly addressed from public information alone. Advisory work brings the same research-oriented approach to those situations. The subject may be a technology decision, an information problem, an operating environment or a larger question involving several parts of an organisation. The work is conducted directly with the people responsible for the matter and results in a considered view of the situation and its implications.

The author

Andrey Bundin is an engineer and researcher working across engineering, industrial systems, technology and decision-making. His professional work has moved between technical systems and the organisations around them: engineering information, industrial IT, solution design, business development and the implementation of systems used in complex operational environments.

Over time, the interesting part has become the space between these layers. A physical asset becomes information in several systems. That information becomes part of an operating process. The process eventually reaches someone responsible for a decision, an investment or an outcome. This perspective shapes the work of Undestry, his independent practice.
